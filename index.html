Study guide · Part 1: The principles

How world events move stock prices

Every headline reaches a stock price through a small number of channels. Learn the channels once, using real cases from 2008 to 2025, and the weekly news starts to make sense.

Lesson 0

One formula, four channels

A share price can be split into two parts: how much the company earns per share (EPS), and how much investors are willing to pay for each unit of those earnings (the P/E ratio).

Price = EPS × P/E. Every piece of news moves the price by changing one or both of these.

News almost never hits EPS or P/E directly. It travels through one of four channels: interest rates, the exchange rate, the economy and profit outlook, and risk appetite.

Swipe sideways to see the whole map →

Example: a strong US inflation report raises expected US interest rates (channel), which lowers the P/E investors will pay (driver), which lowers prices even if company earnings have not changed at all.

Lesson 1

Earnings: the EPS side

Over the long run, prices follow profits. In the short run, what matters most is the outlook for future profits.
Case · Nvidia · 24–25 May 2023

A forecast moved the price more than the result

Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $7.19 billion, down 13% from a year earlier. On its own that was not exciting. But it also said next quarter's revenue would be about $11.0 billion, roughly 50% more, because demand for AI chips was surging. The shares jumped about 24–25% the next day and the company approached a $1 trillion market value.

Revenue: the quarter just reported vs. the next-quarter forecast

US$ billions

Why: investors value a company on the profits they expect in the future. A big upgrade to the outlook raises expected EPS for years ahead, so the price reprices immediately.

Lesson 2

Interest rates: the P/E side

When interest rates rise, the value of future profits falls today, so investors pay a lower P/E. Companies whose profits are far in the future are hit hardest.

Money received in the future is worth less than money today, and the higher the interest rate, the bigger the discount. A safe bond paying 5% also competes with stocks for investors' money.

How much value disappears when rates rise from 1% to 5%

Fall in today's value of ¥100 received in year N. Illustration: pure calculation, not market data.

Profits 20 years away lose about half their value; profits next year barely change. That is why fast-growing technology companies, whose value lies mostly in distant profits, fall the most when rates rise.

Case · United States · 2022

Seven rate hikes in one year

To fight inflation, the US Federal Reserve raised its policy rate from 0–0.25% to 4.25–4.5% in seven steps during 2022.

Lesson 3

Expectations: prices react to surprises

Markets move on the gap between what happened and what was expected. News that everyone expected is already "priced in".

Textbook logic says a rate hike strengthens the currency. But the hike was priced in, and the guidance was softer than feared. The surprise was "dovish", so the yen weakened. Always ask: compared with what was expected?

Lesson 4

The yen: why it matters so much in Japan

A weaker yen raises the yen value of overseas profits, so exporters' EPS goes up. A stronger yen does the opposite.
Case · Toyota · forecast for the year to March 2026

¥1 can mean ¥50 billion

Toyota estimated that each ¥1 move against the dollar changes its operating profit by about ¥50 billion (plus about ¥10 billion per ¥1 against the euro). Its plan assumed ¥146 per dollar.

Rough change in Toyota operating profit if the yen weakens against the dollar

Case · Abenomics · 2013

Policy, a weaker yen and a 57% year

Massive monetary easing under Prime Minister Abe pushed the yen sharply weaker. The Nikkei 225 rose 57% in 2013, its biggest annual gain in more than 40 years, closing the year at 16,291.31.

Case · Yen at a 32-year low · Oct 2022

When the government steps in

With US rates rising fast and Japan's held at zero, plus a record trade deficit from expensive energy imports, the dollar reached ¥151.90 on 21 Oct 2022. Japan's Ministry of Finance bought yen: about ¥3 trillion on 22 Sep and an estimated ¥5 trillion on 21 Oct. The yen briefly jumped to ¥144, but the effect faded within hours.

Lesson: intervention can shock the market for a day, but it rarely reverses a trend driven by interest-rate gaps and trade flows.

Lesson 5

Chain reactions: when everyone exits at once

Moves get extreme when many investors made the same leveraged bet and are forced to unwind it at the same time.
Case · Japan · 31 Jul – 6 Aug 2024

The biggest point drop in Nikkei history, then the biggest rise

31 JulBank of Japan raises rates and hints at more
→
2 AugWeak US jobs report: US rate cuts now expected
→
Rate gap narrowsYen surges from ¥160s (early July) to about ¥144
→
5 AugExporters' EPS outlook cut, bets unwound: Nikkei −4,451

Many investors had borrowed cheap yen to buy higher-yielding assets (often called the "yen carry trade") or had bet on a weak yen. When the yen jumped, they had to sell quickly, which pushed prices down further and forced even more selling.

Largest daily moves in the Nikkei 225

Percent change in one day

The next day the index rose 3,217 points, a record gain, because nothing about Japanese companies' long-term earnings had changed that fast. Forced selling overshoots; the rebound shows it.

Lesson 6

Shocks and recoveries

In a shock, investors first sell everything (risk appetite collapses, P/E falls). How fast prices recover depends on whether earnings are permanently damaged and how strongly governments respond.

A shock caused by a decision can be undone by another decision. That is why policy shocks often recover faster than shocks that destroy real earnings, such as 2008.

Lesson 7

Sectors: the same news, opposite reactions

News that hurts one industry can help another. Ask whose costs go up and whose revenues go up.
Case · Energy · 2022

Oil prices up, oil companies up

After Russia invaded Ukraine, oil and gas prices soared. The same inflation that hurt most companies raised oil producers' profits. The S&P 500 energy sector rose about 58%, the only sector that gained that year, while the index fell about 19%.

Lesson 8

Politics and policy expectations

Markets price what they expect a government to do: spending, taxes, regulation, and pressure on the central bank.

Case · Japan · Oct 2025

The "Takaichi trade"

After Sanae Takaichi won the leadership of the ruling LDP in early October 2025, investors bet on more growth-oriented policy. On 6 Oct the Nikkei rose 2,175 points to a record 47,944, led by defense and semiconductor stocks. On 27 Oct it closed above 50,000 for the first time (50,512.32), helped also by easing US–China trade fears and strong chip-company results.

Case · Japan · 22 Feb 2024

34 years to beat the bubble peak

The Nikkei closed at 39,098.68, finally above the 29 Dec 1989 record of 38,915.87. The trigger that day was strong Nvidia results lifting Japanese chip-equipment makers, with the yen near ¥150 supporting exporters.

Using it every week

Five questions for any headline

  • Which channel? Interest rates, the yen, the economy and profits, or risk appetite?
  • EPS or P/E? Does it change future profits, or what investors will pay for them?
  • Versus what was expected? Look for the forecast, not just the number.
  • Who was crowded into one bet? Crowded, borrowed positions turn small news into big moves.
  • Who wins, who loses? Exporters vs. importers, energy vs. users of energy, growth vs. mature companies.
