MAP

The map: prices move on three levers

Every piece of news reaches a stock price through one of three levers: ① earnings (the power to make money), ② interest rates (the price of money), ③ sentiment (fear of risk).
Tap to open

Every chapter uses this map. When you see a headline, ask “which lever does this move?” That is the first step to reading the market.

CHAPTER 1

A price is “earnings × a multiple for expectations”

Price = EPS (Earnings Per Share) × P/E (Price-to-Earnings ratio: how many years of earnings investors pay for). The price moves only when one of these two moves.
Tap to open
Price = EPS × P/EEPS = profit per share / P/E = the “multiple” the market pays for that profit
Try it: the same company, different prices
EPS moves with the earnings lever; P/E moves with the rates and sentiment levers

Why the multiple changes for the same profit

P/E measures how strongly investors believe the profits will last and grow. Low rates and a bright outlook push P/E up; high rates and fear push it down. In short,EPS = today’s facts, P/E = the price of the future. Most news shakes the P/E side.

CHAPTER 2 · RATES

Why stocks fall when interest rates rise

Future money is valued at a discount to today. Higher rates mean a steeper discount, so companies that earn far in the future fall the most.

Case: 2022, the Fed’s rapid hikes

To fight 40-year-high inflation (US CPI = Consumer Price Index, 9.1% year on year in June), the Fed (Federal Reserve, the US central bank) raised its policy rate from 0.25% to 4.50% in one year. The S&P 500 fell 19.4% for the year (S&P 500 = Standard & Poor's 500, an index of 500 major US companies). The tech-heavy Nasdaq, priced on future growth, fell 33.1%. Just as the simulation above shows: growth companies are the most sensitive to rates.

Quick quiz
A central bank says it will cut rates sooner than expected. Which is most likely to rise?
The answer is B. When the discount weakens, profits far in the future recover the most value today (2022 in reverse).
CHAPTER 3 · EXPECTATIONS

Prices move on the gap from expectations, not on results

Even good news does not move a price if it was already expected (priced in). Only a surprise, a gap from expectations, moves it.
Tap to open

Case: Nvidia’s results, May 2023

In the generative-AI boom, Nvidia guided next-quarter revenue to about $11 billion. Analysts expected about $7.1 billion. This guidance surprise, more than 50% above forecasts, sent the stock up 24% the next day. Its market value passed $1 trillion days later.

The reverse: “great results, falling stock”

Even record profits send a stock down if the market expected more. The question is not “good or bad” but“better or worse than expected”. The line “versus forecasts of …” matters more than “record profit” in the headline.

CHAPTER 4 · CURRENCY

A weak yen helps Japanese stocks, but not always

Exporters earn in dollars and report in yen. When the yen weakens, the same dollar of sales becomes more yen, so earnings (EPS) rise.
Tap to open

Bars: operating profit (¥ trillion) / Labels: average USD/JPY for the year

In the year to March 2024, Toyota became the first Japanese company to earn over ¥5 trillion in operating profit. But in the year to March 2023, profitfell even though the yen weakened, because costly materials and chip shortages outweighed the currency tailwind.Currency is only one of the things that move earnings. That is the accurate way to see it.

Case: 5 Aug 2024, Japan’s “Black Monday”

On 31 July the Bank of Japan raised rates; on 2 August a weak US jobs report narrowed the expected rate gap between the US and Japan. The “yen carry trade”, borrowing cheap yen to buy higher-yielding assets, unwound all at once and the yen surged. The Nikkei fell 4,451 points (−12.4%) in one day, its largest point drop ever, beyond October 1987, then rebounded 3,217 points the next day, its largest point gain ever.

What decides how fast markets recover

Crashes run through the sentiment lever. Recovery speed depended on whether the crisis was inside or outside the financial system, and how fast policy responded.

2008 broke the banks themselves and froze the flow of credit. It took time to see the full losses, and 17 months to reach the bottom.2020 was a shock from outside. Within weeks the Fed cut to zero, offered unlimited liquidity, and governments spent heavily. The fall was fast, but both the rates and sentiment levers were pulled back at once, so the recovery was a record too.

CHAPTER 7 · PRACTICE

Four checks for reading the weekly news

For any headline, ask in this order. This alone explains most moves.
Tap to open
  • R
    Will rates rise or fall?CPI, jobs report, Fed/BOJ meetings → P/E moves (Ch. 2)
  • E
    Did profit forecasts change?Results and guidance → EPS moves; the gap from forecasts matters (Ch. 3)
  • ¥
    Stronger or weaker yen?Driven by the US–Japan rate gap → exporters’ EPS moves (Ch. 4)
  • S
    Is the market scared?VIX (Volatility Index, the “fear gauge”) and chain selling → P/E shrinks for a while (Ch. 5)
  1. The event in one line (e.g. US CPI beat forecasts)
  2. Which lever it moves (rates ↑ → P/E ↓)
  3. The gap from forecasts (if as expected, the move is small)
  4. The actual move and check (if it differs, look for another cause)
CHAPTER 8 · CHECKING REAL TRADES

After an event, when should you have bought, and what?

You can’t predict the event itself, but timing after an event has repeatable clues. We keep the perfect trade in hindsight separate from what was actually possible at the time.
Tap to open

Case 0: US midterm elections, November 2026

In the 3 November midterms, Democrats won both chambers of Congress, leaving the Trump administration facing a divided government. Polls had favored Democrats (about 8 points on the national ballot; one forecast gave Democrats 80% for the House and 57% for the Senate). On the 4th, as results came in, the S&P 500 rose 1.6% and the Dow 3%, the biggest post-midterm gain in 44 years. Trump’s conciliatory tone and an 8.6% drop in Brent oil on hopes the Iran war would end helped. But on the 5th the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, its worst day since April 2025, as AI chip stocks sold off and a semiconductor ETF (Exchange Traded Fund) fell more than 12% in one day.

Why “Foresaw”: the direction was visible in advance

Polls favored Democrats. The Trump administration backed AI investment, while pushback against data centers’ power use was already spreading in some states before the vote.“Democrats win → headwind for AI stocks” could be built from public information before the vote. In fact, from 3 to 6 Nov the AI-heavy Nasdaq fell 3.1% while the Dow fell 1.0%.

But on the 4th, when results came in, everything rose (the Nasdaq too, +1.4%), and AI stocks broke the next day.The direction was readable; the timing was not. So we separate “Best” (exact days) from “Foresaw” (direction only).

Could you really have judged it at the time?
  • ○
    The result (a Democratic win)Polls showed Democrats ahead. The House was nearly priced in; the Senate was close to a toss-up, so winning both removed the last doubt
  • △
    The next-day rally“Stocks rise once results are known” and “divided government suits stocks” are past patterns. But part of the gain came from oil falling on White House remarks that day, which no one could know in advance
  • ×
    The drop two days laterTriggered by fears that a Democratic Congress and state opposition would slow data-center building, plus a Qualcomm sales miss. Linked to the election, but the timing and size were not readable from the result alone
Lesson: you could catch the event rally, but holding on meant a loss two days later, and those who jumped in after the rise lost the most. For event trades, the key is todecide when to sell before you buy.

Hindsight answer vs real-time judgment, event by event

2008 Lehman collapse: when “buy after the event” failed
Buying at the 15 Sep close of 1,192.70 left 907.84 a month later on 15 Oct (−23.9%)

The bottom came six months later, 676.53 on 9 Mar 2009, 43% below the collapse-day close. The cause was inside the banks (the financial system itself), so neither earnings nor sentiment recovered until the full losses were known.When an event is breaking the financial system, buying right after the crash is too early (Ch. 5).

2016 Trump win: the overnight crash was a buying chance
During the count, Dow futures fell over 800 points and S&P futures about 5%. The cash market closed up 1.1% the next day

Hindsight: buy the overnight futures drop. Real time: the promised tax cuts, deregulation and infrastructure spending were positive for earnings. The key was seeing the panic as a temporary move of the sentiment lever.

2022 Russia invades Ukraine: invasion day was the short-term bottom
On 24 Feb the S&P 500 fell to 4,115 intraday, then closed up 1.5% (recovering about 4% within the day)
$1,000 held from the day before the invasion (23 Feb): as of 16 June

Stocks had already fallen on fears of war, so the invasion day became a short-term bottom. Oil (WTI) rose 34% from $92 on 23 Feb to $124 on 8 Mar, but was back at $95 by 16 Mar. But stocks fell again from late March and were lower still by June. Inflation and Fed hikes (Ch. 2) were the bigger lever.

2020 COVID: the buy signal was the central bank, not case counts
The Fed announced unlimited QE on the morning of 23 Mar. That day’s close was the bottom
$1,000 held from the pre-COVID high (19 Feb): as of 23 Apr

Case counts kept rising for months. Stocks bottomed the moment the Fed and government pulled the rates and money-supply levers as far as they would go. Even with bad news continuing, stocks rise first once policy is set. Buying when the WHO declared a pandemic on 11 Mar left you roughly flat a month later.

April 2025 Trump tariff shock: “VIX above 50” was the signal
The S&P 500 fell 12% in four trading days after the 2 Apr tariff announcement, then jumped 9.5% when a 90-day pause was announced on the 9th
$1,000 held at the close of tariff day (2 Apr): as of 2 May

No one could predict the pause. But the VIX rarely tops 50, only in a few moments such as 2008 and 2020, and stocks were higher a year later each time.Judging by how scared the market is, not by what will happen, made this a buy you could actually take at the time.

How to read the ranks
  • BestHitting both the sell day and the buy day. The direction was readable, but the exact days only in hindsight
  • ForesawDecidable at the time from public information (polls, party policies)
  • SkilledPossible at the time if you set the rule (“if X, then Y”) before buying
  • TypicalDo nothing. Anyone can
  • MistakeCommon panic moves made while watching prices
Summary: three signals to use after an event
  • 1
    When uncertainty endsA confirmed vote count, an invasion actually starting. Once “we don’t know what happens” ends, stocks tend to recover (2016, 2022, 2024, day one of 2026). The rebound can last only days
  • 2
    When central banks or governments actRate cuts, liquidity, policy reversals. Often the bottom even while bad news continues (2020, 2025)
  • 3
    When fear overshootsVIX above 40–50, record-size daily drops. Except when the financial system itself is broken, as in 2008 (Ch. 5)

* Every event here eventually recovered. The same rule also lost money at times (early 2008, late 2022). Past results do not guarantee future returns.
